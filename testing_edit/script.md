Every star you see in the night sky will eventually die.
But how a star dies depends on how massive it is.
For millions or even billions of years, stars shine by turning hydrogen into helium, releasing enormous amounts of energy.
But one day, that fuel begins to run out.
Stars like our Sun slowly expand, growing into enormous red giants.
Eventually, they shed their outer layers into space, leaving behind a small, incredibly dense core called a white dwarf.
But much bigger stars have a far more violent ending.
When their fuel runs out, their cores collapse in just seconds.
The star explodes in a massive blast called a supernova.
What remains can become a neutron star, an object so dense that a single teaspoon of its material would weigh billions of tonnes.
But if the collapsing core is massive enough, it forms something even more extreme.
A black hole.
An object with gravity so powerful that not even light can escape.
